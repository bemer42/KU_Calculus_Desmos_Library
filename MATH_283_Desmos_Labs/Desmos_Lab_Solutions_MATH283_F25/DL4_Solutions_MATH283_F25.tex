\question \underline{Parametric Surfaces and Surface Area:} In this part, we'll plot a parametric surface and use Desmos to determine its surface area. 
	
\begin{parts}
	\part Open a new graph in Desmos and save it as ``Parametric Surface."
	\part When defining a parametric surface in Desmos, the two common variables to use are $u$ and $v$.  Define the following three functions on the first three lines: $f(u,v) = u\cos(v) - \frac{u^5}{5} \cos(5v)$, $g(u,v) = -\frac{u^5}{5}\sin(5v) - u\sin(v)$, and $h(u,v) = \frac{2u^3}{3}\cos(3v)$.  On the fourth line, type $(f(u,v), g(u,v), h(u,v))$ and let $u$ range from $0$ to $1.38$ and $v$ from $0$ to $2\pi$.  The generated surface is the same surface as on the front of our textbook (if viewed from above).  
	\part Taking $\boldsymbol{r}(u,v)$ to be this parameterization, in the space below, write out $|\boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v|$.  Then have Desmos calculate $\displaystyle \iint_\mathcal{R} |\boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v|\, dA$.  Report the value below.  What does this number represent? 
	
	\textcolor{red}{Calculating each partial derivative and doing the cross product gives a surprisingly simple result.  We have 
	\begin{align*}
		\boldsymbol{r}_u & = \langle \cos(v) - u^4 \cos(5v), -u^4\sin(5v) - \sin(v), 2u^2 \cos(3v) \rangle \\ 
		\boldsymbol{r}_v & = \langle -u\sin(v) + u^5 \sin(5v), -u^5 \cos(5v) - u \cos(v), -2u^3 \sin(3v) \rangle \end{align*}
	The cross product is 
	\begin{align*}
		\boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v & = \langle 2u^7\sin(3v)\sin(5v) + 2u^3\sin(v)\sin(3v)+2u^7\cos(3v)\cos(5v) + 2u^3\cos(v)\cos(3v), \ldots \\ 
		& \qquad -2u^3 \cos(3v)\sin(v) + 2u^7\cos(3v)\sin(5v) + 2u^3\cos(v)\sin(3v) - 2u^7 \cos(5v)\sin(3v), \ldots \\ 
		& \qquad -u\cos^2(v) + u^9\cos^2(5v) - u\sin^2(v) + u^9\sin^2(5v)\rangle \end{align*}
	We notice that in the first component, we can use the identity $\cos(A-B) = \cos(A)\cos(B) + \sin(A)\sin(B)$, and in the second component, we can use the identity $\sin(A-B) = \sin(A)\cos(B) - \sin(B)\cos(A)$.  We can also use Pythagorean's identity in the third component.  This gives the following: 
	\[ \boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v  = \langle 2(u^3 + u^7)\cos(2v), 2(u^3+u^7)\sin(2v), -u + u^9 \rangle\]
	Computing the length gives the following: 
		\begin{align*}
			|\boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v| & = \sqrt{\big(4(u^3 + u^7)^2\cos^2(2v) + 4(u^3 + u^7)^2 \sin^2(2v) + (-u + u^9)^2 } \\ 
			& = \sqrt{4u^6 + 8u^{10} + 4u^{14} + u^2 - 2u^{10} + u^{18} } \\
			& = \sqrt{u^2 + 4u^6 + 6u^{10} + 4u^{14} + u^{18} }\\
			& = \sqrt{(u^{1/2} + u^{9/2})^4} \\ 
			& = (u^{1/2} + u^{9/2})^2 \\ 
			& = u + 2u^5 + u^9. \end{align*}	
	Therefore, the integral that represents the surface area of the surface is given by 
		\[  \iint_\mathcal{R} |\boldsymbol{r}_u \times \boldsymbol{r}_v|\, dA = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{1.38} (u + 2u^5 + u^9)\, du\, dv \approx 36.187.\] }
